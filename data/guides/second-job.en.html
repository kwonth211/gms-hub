At level 30 you make your second job advancement. In Classic World the second job you pick from the same first job changes your weapon, the maps you can hunt and your potion bill a lot, and players say a bad pick means a rough road to level 70. This guide covers how to advance, a default recommendation per class, weapons, and maps from 30 to 70. Numbers are master-level values.

How the ten second jobs' skills changed is covered separately in the choosing a job guide; this one focuses on "so which one, and where do I go?".

How to advance

At level 30 the second job quest appears. Get a letter from the instructor in your first job town, find the second job instructor, and you're sent to a test map. Collect 30 Dark Marbles from the monsters there and bring them back to choose your second job. The test monsters crowd you more than you'd expect, so bring plenty of potions.

Rules that apply to everyone

Mastery first. Mastery raises your minimum damage, so once you've picked a weapon, levelling mastery first makes your hits-to-kill reliable.

Boosters add two attack speed steps. When you change weapons, look at the weapon's base speed together with the booster. Magicians have no booster, so they judge weapons by magic attack and element.

Final Attack is pressed manually. It no longer swings again on its own: attacks have a 25% chance to enable it, and then you press it yourself within 60 seconds. Put it on a hotkey.

Party buffs. Fighters' Rage boosts weapon attack, Fire/Poison and Ice/Lightning's Meditation boosts magic attack, and thieves' Haste boosts movement.

Warriors: default to a sword Fighter

Two reasons for swords. First, Sword Mastery comes with +20 accuracy. Warriors are blocked from maps by accuracy more often than by damage, so on a first character that accuracy matters. Axe Mastery instead has a 25% chance to inflict a 3-second bleed (100% total). Second, swords have the same multiplier for slashing and stabbing, so damage varies less. Axes and blunt weapons swing and stab at very different multipliers, so if you want reliable one-hit kills, swords are easier.

In parties, the Fighter's Rage gives nearby members +40 attack (and -40 weapon defence). Final Attack hits up to three monsters in front for 200% as a Fighter, one monster for 360% as a Page, and one monster for 350% as a Spearman, who also heals 10% of the damage dealt (capped at 25% of max HP per hit).

All three get Rush: dash 150 pixels forward, hitting up to six monsters for 80% and pushing them back. You can't be knocked back while rushing; it costs 15 MP with a 2-second cooldown. Use it to herd scattered monsters together so Slash Blast and Final Attack hit them all. In old MapleStory this was a fourth job skill.

Spearmen get +20 speed from Polearm Mastery, healing from Final Attack and Iron Will (+50 weapon defence, 35% knockback immunity), suiting players who like to keep distance. The Page's Threaten lowers the speed and weapon defence of up to four monsters.

Weapons and maps. Most level 35-40 weapons are crafted at weapon-crafting stage 8. Classic maps are Fire Boars (32) in the Burnt Land in your early 30s and Zombie Lupins (41) in the monkey forest from 41. If you miss Wraiths (48) or can't kill them in two hits, drop down to Jr. Wraiths.

Magicians: default to Ice/Lightning

Cold Beam has 170 attack, costs 22 MP and slows the target for 3 seconds; it doesn't freeze like it used to. Monsters weak to ice run from Fire Boars to Copper Drakes, Red Drakes and Tauromacis, so Ice/Lightning has an unbroken path of maps to level 70, which is why it's the default. Thunder Bolt hits up to six monsters around you with a 25% chance to stun. It's worth its MP when it hits several monsters; for one or two, Cold Beam is better.

Fire/Poison's Fire Arrow has 180 attack, costs 28 MP and adds a 3-second burn. Against fire-weak monsters such as Ligators, Jr. Wraiths, Cold Eyes and Crocos it does 1.25×. Fire Boars, Drakes, Dark Stone Golems and Red Drakes resist fire, though, so damage drops and burns don't land. That means Fire/Poison can't follow Ice/Lightning's map order. Poison Breath bursts on the first monster it hits, attacks up to six around it and poisons for 5 seconds with a 75% chance (40 MP). The October 30 patch added 10 base damage to Poison Breath and Thunder Bolt.

Clerics can attack undead with Heal, so they shine on undead maps and with a party. Heal shares six target slots, filled first by you and your party, so solo it hits up to five undead. It can't hit ordinary monsters like Horny Mushrooms. Clerics don't get Slow or Meditation.

MP Eater has a 50% chance to drain half the enemy's MP, at most once every 5 seconds. Fire Boars and Ligators have 0 MP, so there's nothing to drain.

Use wands. At the same level a wand has more magic than a staff (level 20 Metal Wand 41 vs Old Wooden Staff 38; level 25 Ice Wand 48 vs Wizard Staff 45). Staves also jump from level 25 straight to 45, with nothing in between. Next targets are the level 35 Wizard Wand (62 magic) and the level 40 Fairy Wand (69 magic). The attack speed printed on a weapon doesn't affect casting speed, so look only at magic.

Maps. At 30, Bubblings in subway Line 1 Area 1 or Stirges in the Transfer Area. Around 40, Ice/Lightning goes to Fire Boars in the Burnt Land, Fire/Poison to Stone Masks in the Excavation Site or the Ant Tunnel. From 50, Ice/Lightning and Fire/Poison do area damage in Ant Tunnel IV, while Clerics take undead maps like the Jr. Wraiths in Line 1 Area 2.

Thieves: default to Assassin

For a first thief, the Assassin's ranged hunting is easier. Claw Mastery raises attack and has a 25% chance to return the star you threw. Critical Throw gives 15% crit chance and 35% crit damage. Star range is 200 base plus 200 from mastered Keen Eyes, 400 pixels in total. Stars cost mesos, so budget both the first set and the cost of recharging.

Drain hits one monster for 240% and has a 12% chance to heal 15% of the damage dealt. At 28 MP it's a healing aid, not a main attack, and heals at most 25% of max HP.

If you want daggers, a hybrid Bandit is the safe route. Savage Blow hits six times from skill level 21, 75% × 6 at master. So many players hunt with first job Lucky Seven maxed (20), then switch to Savage Blow around level 36. Steal hits up to two monsters for 180%; when it succeeds (90%) it stuns for 1.5 seconds and gives +35 attack for 60 seconds. That +35 is counted separately from weapon attack, so it isn't as strong as a dagger with 35 more attack. Don't rush to buy an AP/SP reset (7,000 NX in the Cash Shop) because you already put points elsewhere; only consider it once you actually have points to move.

Haste gives the whole party +30 speed and +10 jump. Assassins restore 10% HP and MP on crits, Bandits on successful dodges, with a 30-second cooldown.

Maps. Stirges in the Transfer Area at 30, Ant Tunnel II at 35; in the 40s, Assassins go to Ant Tunnel Park and the Deep Ant Tunnel while Bandits go to the Burnt Land; from 50, Lupins and Zombie Lupins in the monkey forest.

Bowmen: default to Hunter

For bowmen, look at the map before the job. If monsters pile up on one platform, Hunter; if you can line them up on one floor, Crossbowman. Default to Hunter and pick Crossbowman when the maps suit it.

The Crossbowman's Iron Arrow pierces up to six lined-up monsters (180%, 28 MP). Each monster behind takes 80% of what the one in front took, so the sixth takes about 33% of the first. The subway is where it comes alive: Jr. Wraiths (34) in Line 2 Area 1 and Line 1 Area 2, and Wraiths (48) in Line 2 Area 3, have long horizontal corridors where you can line them up and shoot.

Hunters get 10% crit chance and +10 speed from mastery. A skill both jobs share slows a monster's speed by 35 for 5 seconds on a crit, and with the extra crit chance it triggers more often for Hunters. Final Attack hits up to three monsters for 120% as a Hunter and one for 360% as a Crossbowman.

Weapons. Bows are mostly normal speed (the level 35 Red Viper is fast); crossbows are mostly slow. Boosters make both two steps faster, so until 40 Hunters feel quicker. Soul Arrow removes arrow use but attacks still cost MP, so as Arrow Bomb's MP cost adds up, mana potions get expensive.

Your map decides your potion bill, not your job

For the same job, potion costs vary several times over depending on how many hits you need and how often you get hit. Before moving maps, check accuracy (are you missing?) and whether you kill in one hit. The hits-to-kill calculator does both for every monster, and the training spot finder compares three guide sites' maps for your level.
