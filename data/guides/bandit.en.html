Over 40% of second-job thieves are Bandits. The first question every Bandit asks is "Steal first or Savage Blow first?" A player who simulated it found the answer is neither: Dagger Mastery first. Skill numbers below come from the game data; the time comparisons and gear notes are players' own calculations and reports.

In one look

  • Default order: Haste 1 → Dagger Mastery (maxed at 37) → Savage Blow (47) → Haste (54) → Steal 1 → Nimble Recovery 12 → Steal → leftover into Booster
  • Final build: Dagger Mastery, Savage Blow, Haste and Steal maxed, Nimble Recovery 12, Dagger Booster 9: exactly the 121 points you get by 70.
  • Stats: LUK is your main stat. Pick one dagger line for your next weapon and meet only its STR or DEX requirement.

Skill build: why Mastery first

  1. Level 30: Haste 1 for easier movement
  2. By about 32: Dagger Mastery 5, then Dagger Booster 1
  3. By about 37: Dagger Mastery 20. Meanwhile, grind with the Double Stab you finished in first job: level 1 Savage Blow is only 65% ×4, so there's no reason to switch early.
  4. By about 47: Savage Blow. Six hits unlock at 44 and it's maxed at 47. Switch once it kills in fewer hits than Double Stab.
  5. By about 54: max Haste, then Steal 1 (just to unlock the stun), Nimble Recovery 12, Steal 30, leftover into Booster.

One player simulated levels 30 to 70 1,500 times per build. Compared with Mastery first, Steal first took 5 to 16% longer and Savage first 8 to 10% longer.

The other road: Mastery, then Steal, grinding with Double Stab. The same simulation found it 8 to 10% faster than the default, because it spends less MP and Steal's buff adds 35 attack. The trade-off: Double Stab hits weaker than Savage, often needs one more hit, and has shorter reach. Many say it works better than expected if you stun two monsters from a distance with Steal and then close in. If fighting up close suits you, take this road; if you'd rather hit from a little further, take the default.

Reach and the Steal buff

  • Double Stab: 70 to 84 pixels forward, almost no height since it's a thrust. Stun with Steal from range, then close in.
  • Savage Blow: 95 pixels forward and up to 62 pixels above your feet. On maps full of slopes and stairs it also hits monsters slightly above you, which is much more comfortable.
  • Steal's attack +35: a separate attack stat, not weapon attack. One player's maths: with a level 40 Gephart it's worth about 8 weapon attack, about 14% damage, and about 8% at level 70.

  • The STR line (Cass, Korean Fan, Bazlud) is very fast: with Booster one Savage Blow takes 0.66 seconds, against 0.72 for the DEX line like Gephart. That's 9% more hits in the same time.
  • Korean Fan only needs LUK 70, so even a Bandit with no STR or DEX can use it.
  • Players report Gephart can be crafted at weapon crafting level 8 from steel, mithril and screws. Compare the finished item's price with the materials.
  • The level 50 Shinkita isn't on the crafting list; it only drops, and it's pricey. Hold out with Gephart and switch when a fair listing shows up. Check prices on the live market.
  • Stat warning: raising both STR and DEX for weapons you won't use costs LUK. Pick your next dagger line and meet only its requirement. See the Thief equipment list for each level.

Where to train by level

Bandits fight up close: run a short loop on one platform, clear it with Savage Blow, then move to the next.

If you're killing fast where you are, there's no need to move. Before moving, put your stats and dagger into the hits-to-kill calculator and check that your minimum damage covers the monster's HP and your accuracy is enough. When a map is crowded, a place where you one-shot everything and can find a spot beats chasing higher monsters.

In short

Not Steal first, not Savage first: finish Mastery, hold out with Double Stab, then move to Savage Blow is the fastest on paper. If you like fighting up close, the Steal plus Double Stab road works too. Pick a map for your level from the Bandit training routes.
