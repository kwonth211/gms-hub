The biggest trap when picking a class in Classic World is remembering how it used to play. Many skills kept their names but changed what they do, and some moved to a different job advancement entirely. This guide covers the ten first and second jobs available now, focusing on what changed from pre-Big Bang MapleStory.

Numbers are master-level values from the skill descriptions at launch. Classic World also shows attack and calculates damage differently from the old game, so don't call a skill "nerfed" from one number alone.

Warriors: what changed for all of them

The first-job skills already feel different. HP recovery now also boosts what potions and other recovery items heal, not just regeneration while standing still. With potion costs as high as they are in Classic World, that matters more than it sounds. Max HP Increase raises your max HP by a percentage instead of adding HP per level-up, so the old rule of "max it first or lose HP forever" no longer holds.

From second job all three use Rush, and Final Attack is an attack you use directly. It hits a different number of targets with a different extra effect for each job.

Fighter

Uses swords and axes, and the mastery bonus depends on the weapon: swords give mastery and accuracy, axes give mastery and a chance to cause bleeding. Bleed is damage over time, so it does little against monsters that die in one or two hits. Final Attack hits up to three monsters in front of you.

The signature buff, Rage, raises nearby party members' attack and lowers their weapon defence (+40 attack, -40 defence at master). Don't read that 40 as 40 weapon attack in the old sense. The old Fighter's Power Guard isn't in second job. You still hunt mainly with Power Strike and Slash Blast, which can feel repetitive if you like new attacks arriving every few levels.

Page

Uses swords and blunt weapons. Sword Mastery adds accuracy like the Fighter's; Blunt Mastery adds critical damage (+20% at master). That is 20% on crits, not 20% overall, so how it feels depends on how often you crit. Final Attack hits one monster.

The Page's identity is Threaten: lowering enemies' attack and defence to make fights easier. There is no attack buff like Rage. Rather than picking Page for the elemental charges that come later, check that you enjoy how it hunts at second job now.

Spearman

The most changed class. Second job has no Hyper Body; it moved to third job (Dragon Knight). If you're picking Spearman to boost the party's HP and MP from level 30 as in the old days, you'll be disappointed.

Instead, Spear Mastery has a chance to ignore weapon defence and Polearm Mastery adds movement speed. Final Attack hits one monster and drains HP, so unlike the other warriors you heal while attacking. Iron Will is self-only and adds weapon defence and a chance to ignore knockback (35% at master).

Magicians: what changed for all of them

MP recovery also boosts item recovery, and Max MP Increase works by percentage (+25% at master). Magic Claw hits one monster twice. Magic Guard is now a toggle, so you don't recast it on a timer, but part of the damage you take still comes out of MP, so MP management still matters. Magic Armor adds 120 weapon and magic defence for 600 seconds.

Skills shared by all three second jobs changed too. MP Eater triggers at most once every 5 seconds instead of on every hit. Slow lowers avoidability as well as movement speed, which makes it an accuracy aid too.

Fire/Poison wizard

Fire Arrow hits one monster with fire and adds a 3-second burn (180 attack at master). The key skill is Poison Breath: it bursts where it lands, hits up to six monsters around that point and has a chance to poison them. The October 30 patch raised its base damage by 10, along with Thunder Bolt. The old idea that Fire/Poison kills one monster at a time at second job no longer fits. Because the poison spreads from the impact point, watch where monsters bunch up and where your projectile lands.

Ice/Lightning wizard

Cold Beam hits one monster with ice and slows it instead of freezing it. Thunder Bolt drops lightning around you, hitting several monsters with a chance to stun. Damage plus crowd control is its strength. Because it hits around you, monsters tend to get close, and Thunder Bolt's MP cost needs watching.

Cleric

Heal restores nearby party members and damages undead. Invincible cuts the weapon damage you take (20% at master) and damages monsters that touch you. The big change is Bless: instead of the old defence, accuracy and avoid buff, it reduces all damage nearby party members take by 15% and raises Heal's recovery by 10%. Judging a Cleric by Heal alone misses half of what it brings. Holy Arrow is single-target, not an area attack.

Bowmen: Hunter and Crossbowman

At first job, Eye of Amazon extends bow and crossbow range and Focus raises accuracy and avoidability.

Hunter

Bow Mastery adds a slow on crits and Final Attack hits up to three monsters. The main attack is Arrow Bomb: an exploding arrow that hits up to six monsters with a chance to stun. Some older test notes list four targets, but at launch it is six. If you want to fight groups from range with stuns and slows, Hunter fits.

Crossbowman

Crossbow Mastery raises critical damage and Final Attack hits one monster. The main attack is Iron Arrow, which pierces up to six monsters in a straight line, dealing less to each one further back. It shines on long straight maps where monsters line up and struggles on maps where they scatter.

Thieves: Assassin and Bandit

At first job, Nimble Body raises accuracy and avoidability and Keen Eyes extends throwing-star range. Double Stab hits one monster twice with a dagger. Disorder lowers an enemy's attack and weapon defence, and Dark Sight is now a toggle. You can't attack while hidden, so it is for moving around or escaping danger.

Assassin

Critical Throw gives 15% crit chance and 35% crit damage at master. Endure is replaced by Critical Recovery: crits have a chance to restore HP and MP, with a 30-second cooldown after each trigger. Drain heals at most 25% of your max HP per hit. Haste gives +30 speed and +10 jump, less than the old 40 and 20. There's no real area attack at second job, so you play mobile and kill one monster at a time rather than sweeping groups.

Bandit

Dagger Mastery raises crit chance and the chance to ignore knockback. Endure is replaced by Nimble Recovery: dodging has a chance to restore HP and MP (also with a 30-second cooldown). The skill to watch is Steal. It hits up to two monsters, and a successful steal briefly stuns the target and gives you +35 attack for 60 seconds. It succeeds only once per monster, so keeping the buff up means switching targets. Savage Blow hits one monster six times at 75% each: six hits, not six monsters.

So which one?

There's no single best class right now. Third job and beyond aren't out yet, and too much has changed to carry old tier lists over. These rules of thumb lead to fewer regrets:

  • Clear groups fast on your own: Hunter, Fire/Poison or Ice/Lightning. All three hit several monsters from second job.
  • A clear role in parties: Cleric (Bless and Heal) or Fighter (Rage).
  • Melee that feels good: Bandit (Steal buff), Page (Threaten), Spearman (HP drain).
  • Waiting for the old Spearman's Hyper Body: that means waiting for third job.

Once you've chosen, use the level 1-30 leveling route for class-appropriate maps, and the hits-to-kill calculator to see which monsters your build kills fastest.
