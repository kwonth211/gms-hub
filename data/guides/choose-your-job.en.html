For a first character, play a Thief (Assassin) to hunt alone and make mesos, a Magician (Cleric) to heal in parties, or a Warrior to fight up close and hit groups. Want range without spending much on ammo? Play a Bowman. Find the play style you want in the table below and it names the class.

What each family is like up to level 30

Warrior: the hardest first job, much easier after 30

Your first-job attacks are Power Strike (160% → 260%, one target) and Slash Blast, which costs 8 to 16 HP per use. Melee means standing next to monsters, so you take a lot of hits. Iron Body adds 20–40% weapon defense and makes those hits hurt less.

At 30 the second job brings Rush (six targets), a Booster and, for Fighters, Rage, and training gets much smoother. Pick Warrior if you can push through first job. Get accuracy from Precise Strikes (+5 to +20 accuracy) rather than DEX.

Magician: MP is money

You kill from range with Energy Bolt (90 → 130 magic attack) or Magic Claw (45 → 65, two hits). Each cast costs 8–20 MP, so you keep buying Blue Potions ({{price:2000003}}) and Lemons ({{price:2010005}}). Magic Guard moves part of the damage you take onto MP and saves HP potions.

None of the three second-job magicians has a Booster, so they attack slowly. They get Teleport instead, and hitting an element weakness raises damage sharply. Ice/Lightning and Fire/Poison pick their maps by monster weakness.

Bowman: long range, cheap ammo

Arrow Blow reaches 350 and deals 160% → 240%. Arrows run about {{price:2060000}} for bows and {{price:2061000}} for crossbows on the Free Market, far cheaper than stars. Focus and The Eye of Amazon raise accuracy and range.

The downside: once monsters reach you, your rhythm breaks, and platforms block arrows. Shooting while jumping is awkward too, so claim a spot to stand and shoot first.

Thief: the easiest class to level alone

Lucky Seven deals 60% → 140% for just 8–16 MP. You can throw mid-jump, and high avoidability means fewer hits taken. The second-job Assassin's Critical Recovery, once maxed, restores 10% of max HP and MP every 30 seconds.

Stars are the big expense. Right now Subi stars cost {{price:2070000}}, Wolbi {{price:2070001}} and Mokbi {{price:2070002}}. To skip that cost, go Bandit with a dagger and take Double Stab from first job.

Solo or with a party

If you play short sessions or alone, pick a class with plenty of solo spots. Assassins solo Stirges in the Transfer Area from 20 to 35, and Hunters and Fire/Poison mages solo Ligators in the Swamp of Despair from 30 to 35. Ligators are slow and rarely reach a ranged class.

A regular party opens party-only spots: the Kerning Party Quest (from 21, four players), Lorang Lorang (a two-hit party map) and Downstairs at the Forest (a party around 20 plus one player near 35). Clerics earn EXP when party members kill monsters they healed in the Ant Tunnel, so they level faster the more party time they get.

  • Stirge Lv 20 · Assassin solo
  • Ligator Lv 31 · Hunter, F/P solo
  • Zombie Mushroom Lv 24 · Cleric party
  • Evil Eye Lv 27 · Fighter, Bandit

When you look for a party, state your damage and which floor you can hold, not just your job. The training spot finder splits solo and party spots for your level and class.

Common mistakes when picking a class

  • Picking for a future third or fourth job. Only the first two jobs exist right now. Choose a job you'll enjoy with the skills you can learn today, all the way to 70.
  • Picking from high-level footage. Those clips include scrolled weapons, buffs and party members. Look at how many hits a kill takes at your level with plain gear.
  • Leaving supplies out of the math. Stars and MP potions are a daily expense. With little starting money, the Bowman or the dagger Bandit costs less.
  • Quitting Warrior during first job. Rush, Booster and Rage arrive at 30 and change everything. Get through first job with Iron Body and Improved HP Recovery.
  • Going Cleric solo on Heal alone. Solo Clerics hit their slowest stretch at 42–48. To play alone, pick Ice/Lightning or Fire/Poison.

Frequently asked questions

Which class is best for solo play?

The Assassin. It throws while jumping and dodges a lot, so it uses fewer potions, and it has plenty of solo spots. Fire/Poison comes next, hunting monsters weak to fire.

Ice/Lightning or Fire/Poison?

Ice/Lightning to hit groups, Fire/Poison to hit single targets hard. Ice/Lightning's Thunder Bolt hits six and it shreds ice-weak Fire Boars. Fire/Poison handles fire-weak Ligators and Jr. Wraiths, but Fire Boars and Drakes resist fire.

Hunter or Crossbowman?

Hunters hit and stun six monsters with Arrow Bomb, and bows attack faster than crossbows. Crossbowmen pierce six with Iron Arrow, which is strongest against monsters lined up on long flat platforms. At 30 the Hunter uses the Ryden (50 attack) and the Crossbowman the Eagle Crow (52).

Should a Spearman use a spear or a polearm?

Most level with a polearm to 49 and switch to a spear at 50. Switching means re-spending Mastery, Booster and Final Attack, so you need an SP Reset scroll ({{price:5051001}}). Polearm Mastery adds up to +20 speed, and Spear Mastery adds 1% → 20% defense ignore.

Is Warrior worth playing?

Yes, if you get through to 30. First job is the hardest because you fight up close one target at a time, but Rush, Final Attack and Booster at second job let you clear groups. In first job you can also skip Slash Blast and tank with Iron Body.

Is there a third job?

Not yet. Only the first two jobs are open, so plan around filling your second-job skills by level 70.

Can a Cleric level alone?

It can, but levels 42–48 are the slowest. Solo, max Invincible first and kill undead (Zombie Mushroom, Jr. Wraith, Zombie Lupin) with Holy Arrow. Switch to heal-hunting once you find a party.
