Our scroll records showed why copying the middle asking price can leave a listing waiting. You are adding another offer to the middle of stock still on sale. Read both sold marks and the cheaper offers available now to see where a buyer has alternatives.

You matched other shops, but the scroll stayed put

Looking at nearby prices is a natural way to avoid listing too high. But the prices you can see are not all prices that sold. Unsold offers remain visible too.

Grouping our recent Gloves Attack records showed a large gap in sold-mark shares around 1.80m. The band below it had many more sold marks than the band above. An asking median alone would not reveal that difference.

When you want to sell and buy your next upgrade, start with that gap. Instead of leaving the price untouched because other shops ask the same, move toward the offers a buyer sees first.

Every 100k cut does not move you forward equally

If lowering the price feels wasteful, do not jump straight to the cheapest offer. Count what sits ahead at each price. At the saved time, 161 listings were cheaper than 1.80m, but only four were cheaper than 1.70m. That 100k reduction crossed a crowded part of the market.

Another reduction from 1.70m to 1.60m only changed the cheaper count from four to one. The same amount given up buys a different move forward. Start around 1.70m if you can wait, and go lower when clearing immediately matters more.

This is the useful selling insight in the data: look at how many cheaper offers you move past for the money you give up. It helps you find a place near the front without automatically matching the lowest price.

Do not let a price target stall the next equipment purchase

If selling five scrolls will pay for a claw, put the money you would receive beside the amount you need. At 2m each, the total is 10m. At 1.70m, it is 8.50m. Decide what waiting for the extra 1.50m does for your equipment plan.

If 8.50m buys the needed gear and improves hunting now, clear the scrolls first. If no purchase is urgent, watch the cheaper supply thin while you wait. A clear reason to wait is better than reopening the same unsold stock at the same price without a plan.

A low purchase cost does not turn the highest asking price into your profit. Plan the next purchase around what you receive. If your next thought is “I can just use the scrolls,” read the Work Gloves scrolling story first. Their sale value is money you commit when you use them.

The saved 1.70m price is not a permanent listing instruction. New offers arrive and cheap ones disappear. Take the order of decisions from this article: look at the offers ahead of you, then set a price that serves your next purchase.
