After advancement, compare accuracy, casts per kill, party coverage and supplies in that order. A newly learned skill does not automatically justify a harder map.

The thirties: build a reliable baseline

Melee classes can compare KPQ and one-hit mushrooms before testing Evil Eye or Burnt Land. Assassins should check misses after upgrading from Stirge routes. Hunters can compare Ligator and Deep Ant Tunnel. Fire/Poison users should compare Fire Arrow against their old attack on suitable targets. Other Magicians should keep their earlier route until the new attack is efficient.

The forties: Lorang or an existing route

Lorang requires short kills and a party assignment. Compare floor-specific damage taken rather than treating upgraded equipment footage as a starter setup. Sleepy Dungeon II needs manageable Lupin and Curse Eye kills, including the time to climb back. Fire Arrow users must check elemental matchups and terrain separately.

The fifties: accuracy is a recurring expense

Wraith can expose accuracy problems. If potions solve misses, their price remains part of every session. Early Dark Stone Golem attempts can combine buff costs, long kills and contact damage; this is an advanced trial rather than a low-budget default.

The sixties: preserve party coverage

Compare Tomb III and Camp I floor assignments for claw users. Magicians must evaluate their actual damage skill; Clerics should not assume Heal-only grinding. Warriors can compare compact Curse Eye and Monkey Forest routes. Reassess when a party member leaves.

Above 70 and session records

Do not pull high-level Kargo or Sanctuary footage into an early route. Better skills, equipment and recovery are part of those setups. Faster kills on an older map may be the best next step.

  • Record EXP during equal ten-minute sessions including wait time.
  • Track casts, misses and travel downtime.
  • Count consumed potions, buffs, ammunition and travel.
  • Separate realized sales from unsold rare items.
