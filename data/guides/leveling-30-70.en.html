Past level 30, picking a map suddenly gets hard. Accuracy requirements jump as monster levels rise, and Victoria Island has fewer worthwhile maps, so this is where the fight for spots is fiercest. For each level band this guide lists the well-known standard, a less crowded alternative and where to go when both are full.

Levels 1-30 are covered in the level 1-30 leveling route, and default picks per class in the second job guide.

Two things to check before picking a map

Accuracy. Classic World's hit formula includes your level, so the "95 accuracy" guides quote usually means at the same level as the monster. Monsters above your level need more, and if you miss, a great EXP table means nothing. Short on accuracy? Pick monsters with low avoidability for their level.

Hits to kill. If your minimum damage is at least the monster's HP, it dies in one hit every time; if two minimum hits beat its HP, it's a reliable two-hit kill. One big crit is not the same as a one-hit kill every time, and a new weapon that keeps a two-hit kill at two hits barely changes your speed. The hits-to-kill calculator checks both against every monster.

Levels 30-36: KPQ and the swamp

Unlike the old days, Classic World's Kerning party quest has no upper level limit. "Gator runs", where you keep killing the Ligators that respawn in stage 1, give the best EXP but cost potions; parties that run through the bonus stage still come out ahead on mesos after potions. How to run it is in the Kerning party quest guide.

If you'd rather not party, Ligators in the Kerning City swamp are fine too. They're slow, so you get hit less and spend less on potions. Around 35, Jr. Wraiths in the subway are an option, but if you skipped accuracy skills at first job you'll miss, so go a little later.

Levels 36-45: Lorangs instead of Fire Boars

The standards are Fire Boars in the Burnt Land and Zombie Lupins (41) in the monkey forest. Both are famous and both are crowded: players ask whether Fire Boar spots are "reserved", and Zombie Lupins use magic attacks that eat potions.

For a quieter spot, try Lorangs (38). Head from Lith Harbor toward Florina Beach and go past the hut on the shore. They have low avoidability for their level, so 79 accuracy at the same level is enough, and they spawn in large numbers, so there are monsters left even with several players around. The map is big enough for a six-person party, and they're weak to lightning, which makes them especially comfortable for Ice/Lightning.

If that's full too, move to Curse Eyes in the tree dungeon of the northern forest. Players say you earn more than you spend on potions there and spots are easier to find.

The monkey forest: each floor has a role

The monkey forest is the busiest area right now. Zombie Lupins need 95 accuracy at level 41, and a claw thief who kills them in two hits barely uses potions unless they bump into them, so mesos pile up. One recorded claw-thief party made about 12,000 EXP every 5 minutes and 60,000-70,000 mesos an hour.

In parties, the strongest player takes the top floor, the weakest takes floor 3 where few monsters spawn (the "cleanup" spot), and the rest take floors 1 and 2. You climb ropes a lot, so movement speed helps, and ranged classes have the edge over melee.

Map 1 gives the most EXP but is crowded with overlapping parties. Map 2, with a little less EXP and fewer people, can be more comfortable. A level 55 claw thief who measured map 2 recorded a modest 110,000 EXP per hour but the most mesos, about 70,000.

Levels 45-55: Wraiths, or the tomb when they're full

The standard is Wraiths (48) in subway Line 2 Areas 2 and 3. They're still first choice because no map in Victoria spawns more monsters. But they need about 104 accuracy at the same level, so melee classes miss a lot. Everyone in this band crowds in, and one report says a level 48 Cleric killing them in two hits still lost 200 mesos a minute after potions.

The alternative is the tomb deep inside the Perion Excavation Site, home to level 44 Skeledogs and level 47 Mummydogs. They need 89 and 95 accuracy at the same level, much less than Wraiths. Map 3 is mostly Mummydogs with heavy spawns. Both are undead and take 25% more from holy attacks, which suits Clerics, and the simple two-floor layout draws claw thieves, warriors and mages too.

If that's full as well, there are Wraiths in Line 1 Area 4. Drakes in the valley are crowded and their EXP dropped from 142 to 133, so they're a lower priority now.

Levels 55-70: Sleepywood Dungeon 4 instead of 5, and the camp

Sleepywood Dungeon map 5, the famous level 55-70 standard, isn't recommended any more. A patch cut its spawn from 31 monsters to 20 and slowed respawns. The map is wide but sparse, so it no longer lives up to its name.

Use Sleepywood Dungeon map 4, right before it. It's mostly level 58 Dark Stone Golems and wasn't on the spawn-cut list. The map is small, so monsters gather on one screen. Golems need about 100 accuracy at the same level and drop Tobi Throwing Stars, which can be worth a lot with luck. They hit harder than you'd think, though, so potions can outrun your earnings; go when you're confident about managing HP.

There's also the new Excavation Site camp. In camp map 1, 20 of the 25 monsters are level 57 Skeleton Soldiers, so about 100 accuracy does it, and one data site estimates about 340,000 EXP per hour, near the top for this range. Four-person parties taking one floor each are common, and it's steady enough that players past level 80 still hunt there. After about level 60, move to camp map 3, full of level 63 Officer Skeletons; it needs 120 accuracy. The camp is all undead, so holy attacks do well.
