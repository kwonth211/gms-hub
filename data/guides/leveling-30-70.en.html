After advancing, look at accuracy, casts per kill, party coverage and supplies, in that order. A newly learned skill does not automatically justify a harder map.

The thirties: build a reliable baseline

Melee classes stay on KPQ and one-hit mushrooms, then move to Evil Eye or Burnt Land. Assassins should check misses after upgrading from Stirge routes. Hunters go to Ligator, then Deep Ant Tunnel. Fire/Poison users switch to Fire Arrow on targets where it kills in fewer casts than their old attack. Other Magicians should keep their earlier route until the new attack is efficient.

The forties: Lorang or an existing route

Lorang requires short kills and a party assignment. Each floor hits you differently, and footage with upgraded equipment is not a starter setup. Sleepy Dungeon II needs manageable Lupin and Curse Eye kills, including the time to climb back. Fire Arrow users must check elemental matchups and terrain separately.

The fifties: accuracy is a recurring expense

Wraith can expose accuracy problems. If potions solve misses, their price remains part of every session. Early Dark Stone Golem attempts pile up buff costs, long kills and contact damage; go later, with money to spare.

The sixties: preserve party coverage

Claw users split floors at Tomb III or Camp I. Magicians go by their real damage skill; Heal-only grinding does not carry a Cleric. Warriors use the compact Curse Eye and Monkey Forest routes. Reassess when a party member leaves.

Above 70 and session records

Do not pull high-level Kargo or Sanctuary footage into an early route. Better skills, equipment and recovery are part of those setups. Faster kills on an older map are often the best next step.

  • Record EXP during equal ten-minute sessions including wait time.
  • Track casts, misses and travel downtime.
  • Count consumed potions, buffs, ammunition and travel.
  • Separate realized sales from unsold rare items.
