Two magicians of the same level: one is drowning in mana potion costs, the other hunts comfortably. The difference usually isn't expensive gear but which skills you use where, and which monsters you hunt. This guide covers stats, first job SP, second job SP order for Ice/Lightning, Fire/Poison and Cleric, maps for each, and how to think about potion costs. For choosing a job, read the second job guide first.

Stats: INT first, LUK only for your weapon

Start from STR 4, DEX 4, INT 12, LUK 5. INT drives your damage: in Classic World every 2 INT adds 1 magic, and INT also enters the spell damage formula separately.

Rather than never adding LUK, we suggest matching the LUK requirement of the weapon you'll wear next, minus the LUK from your gear. The level 40 Fairy Wand needs 30 LUK and the level 50 Cromi needs 40. Level 55 weapons need 50, so if you stop at 45 by eye, you can't equip the weapon without gear bonuses.

If you keep missing higher-level monsters, check the level gap and accuracy before adding more LUK. First see whether an accuracy potion solves it or a monster one step lower is better.

Numbers worth knowing

  • Weaknesses are 1.25×, not the old 1.5×. If hitting a weakness doesn't cut a hit from the kill, it barely shows.
  • Energy Bolt vs Magic Claw. At max level both total 130 power. Bolt costs 16 MP and Claw 20, so Bolt uses 20% less. But Bolt is a projectile that terrain blocks, while Claw hits through it. On maps with lots of platforms, Claw is easier.
  • Magic Guard moves part of the damage you take onto MP (30% at level 1, up to 80%). You die less but spend more MP. Magic Armor adds 40 weapon and magic defence at level 1, up to 120.
  • MP recovery boosts potion healing by 10% at level 1, up to 25%. Max MP Increase no longer punishes you for not taking it early.
  • Teleport costs 55 MP at level 1, dropping to 15 at max, and its distance grows from 110 to 150. Spamming it as soon as you learn it costs more than you'd think.

First job SP: the economical build

For ordinary hunting we suggest Energy Bolt 20, Magic Guard 3, MP recovery 15, Max MP Increase 3, the rest in Magic Armor. If terrain makes you use Claw: Bolt 1, Claw 20, Guard 3, recovery 15, Max MP 2, the rest in Armor. If you need to survive more, move some Armor points to Guard.

At level 10, advance with Grendel the Really Old in the Ellinia library. Start with monsters that die in few hits, such as snails, slimes and pigs, then look at Stirges in the subway Transfer Area and Bubblings in Line 1 Area 1. If there's no room or flying monsters annoy you, try the tree dungeon in the northern forest. From 21, the Kerning party quest is an option too.

How to think about potion costs

At regular shop prices, mana potions large and small cost roughly 1 meso per MP. Bigger isn't automatically cheaper, and once you count the recovery wasted by overfilling, it can be worse.

With Magic Guard maxed, taking 100 damage means refilling 20 HP and 80 MP. That can cost more than refilling 100 HP with Red Potions, so think of Guard as trading mesos for survival.

Magic potions cost 500 mesos in shops for +10 magic. Use them only if they turn a three-hit kill into two. Don't confuse them with attack potions: magicians need magic.

Ice/Lightning: Cold Beam for one, Thunder Bolt for groups

Cold Beam hits one monster with ice: 110 power at level 1 (11 MP), 170 at max (22 MP). It doesn't freeze like it used to; it slows for 3 seconds. Thunder Bolt hits up to six monsters around you, and the October 30 patch raised it to 50 at level 1 and 80 at max (40 MP at max).

Against a single monster, Cold Beam is 170 power for 22 MP and Thunder Bolt 80 for 40 MP. A map full of monsters isn't the same as hitting several at once, so to solo steadily and save mesos, level Cold Beam first; on dense maps or with a fixed party, Thunder Bolt first is easier.

Meditation unlocks with 3 points in MP Eater and gives the party up to +25 magic. MP Eater triggers with up to a 50% chance, at most once every 5 seconds, and can't drain monsters with no MP, like Fire Boars.

Maps. Compare Fire Boars, weak to ice, in the Burnt Land (map 3 if map 1 is busy), the valley with Copper Drakes, Lorangs and Clangs that group up nicely for Thunder Bolt, and Curse Eyes in the northern forest tree dungeon maps 8 and 9. Copper Drakes have 2,379 HP, so more damage that doesn't cut a hit won't speed you up as much as you'd hope.

Fire/Poison: single target on fire-weak monsters, plus poison bursts

Fire Arrow has 120 power at level 1 (14 MP) and 180 at max (28 MP), plus a burn. Poison Breath now bursts where the poison lands, hitting up to six monsters around it, with 60 direct power at level 1 and 90 at max. For mostly single-target hunting, take Teleport 1, finish Fire Arrow, then poison and Meditation; if you've secured a spot where monsters bunch up, poison first is fine.

Fire Arrow is a projectile too, so if you can see a monster but terrain blocks the shot, change where you stand before blaming your magic.

Maps. Jr. Wraiths and Ligators, both weak to fire, are the classics. Jr. Wraiths have MP for MP Eater; Ligators don't. If the subway is busy, try Kerning City swamp maps 1 and 2, and later Cold Eyes. On the other hand Curse Eyes and golems resist poison well, and Commander Skeletons are immune to poison. Rather than forcing poison, move to another monster when it doesn't work.

Cleric: check whether it's undead

Holy Arrow is a single-target holy arrow: 100 power at level 1 (14 MP), 160 at max (28 MP). One approach is to level this before Heal so you can keep hunting ordinary monsters. Heal widens from level 16 and costs 27 MP at max. You, your party and undead monsters share the same target slots, so it works differently solo and in a party.

The basic build opens Heal, Teleport and MP Eater, then levels Holy Arrow. For party play take Bless; if getting hit solo is the problem, take Invincible first. At max, Invincible cuts the weapon damage you take by 20%, and Bless cuts damage taken by nearby party members by 15%.

Remember: being weak to holy doesn't mean Heal can hit it. Dark Stone Golems are weak to holy but aren't undead, so Heal does nothing. Undead you can Heal-hunt include Jr. Wraiths, Zombie Lupins, Wraiths, and the Skeledogs and Mummydogs in the Excavation Site tomb. The tomb monsters have no MP, so MP Eater can't refill you there. Zombie Lupins attack from range, so don't ignore incoming damage just because you have Heal.

When to change weapons

Upgrade because it cuts a hit from your kills, not because you reached the level. Turning a shaky two-hit kill into a reliable one-hit kill is worth a lot; if the count doesn't change, wait and spend on potions and armour instead. For magician gloves, look for magic scrolls, not attack. The attack speed on wands and staves doesn't affect casting, so compare magic only. Both wands and staves can be used with a shield.

After level 70

The level cap is 100 for now and there's no third job, so past 70 you still hunt with second job skills. All three jobs need 140 points to max second job, so your second job skills are done by level 77. From 90, each level needs about 2.1 times the EXP of the previous one, so building efficiency you can sustain comes first. Ice/Lightning should compare grouping lower monsters against forcing lightning-resistant Tauros; Fire/Poison should look at fire-weak single targets like Cold Eyes and Crocos; Clerics should compare Wraiths with the tomb's undead. Maps by level band are in the level 30-70 training guide, and the hits-to-kill calculator shows how each spell does against every monster.
