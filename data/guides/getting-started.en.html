If you just started Global MapleStory Classic World, memories of old-school Maple only get you so far. Some menus work differently, the chat is full of trade shorthand, and one keybind can change how fast you get through quests. These are the things worth knowing on day one, picked because they save real time.

Settings worth changing on day one

Turn on the third quickslot row. Open the key settings and tick the option that enables a third quickslot row. Potions, buffs and a return scroll fill the default slots fast.

Use Y, not Space, to talk to NPCs. Space pauses at the quest-accept window; Y goes through the dialogue and accepts in one go. It adds up on long quest chains and party quests.

Pet potion settings live in the Deco tab. The free quest pet doesn't show its potion setting where you'd expect, so it is easy to think it has none; it's inside the Deco tab of the pet window. The basic pet may not even pick up items right in front of it, so pet skills like auto-loot and a wider pickup range are bought separately from the Cash Shop. Most cash items can move between characters on the same account, so a purchase carries over to your alts.

Stats: your beginner AP is not reset at first job

This trips up more new players than anything else. AP you put in as a Beginner stays after your first job advancement. First job is at level 10 with no stat requirement, so once you have picked a class, most players put AP into that class's main stat from the start.

How much DEX you need for accuracy depends on the class. Ranged classes such as thieves using claws often keep DEX close to their level, while mages put almost everything into INT. If you made a mistake, the AP Reset scroll in the Cash Shop (7,000 NX) works from level 10. Skill differences by class are covered in the choosing a job guide.

Leaving Maple Island

Maple Island has 10 quests, and finishing them usually puts you around level 8. From level 7, Shanks at the dock takes you to Lith Harbor for 300 mesos. It's one-way: you can't come back. Collect the island-only rewards before you leave, such as the chair from Pio's quest that refills HP while you sit. If you missed it, the shop in Lith Harbor sells a chair for 1,000 mesos.

Why a party beats soloing

Hunting in a party adds a party bonus on top of everyone's share, which is why most players recommend partying except on a few maps. Ask in chat, or start a party yourself and invite people on the same map.

EXP is split differently from the old days. For each monster, 80% is shared by the members' level ratio and 20% by how much damage each member dealt. Each extra member adds 10% to your share, up to 30%. Two players of the same level end up close to 60/40 even if one does nearly all the killing.

One catch: a member who hasn't attacked, been hit or used a skill for 10 seconds is dropped from the EXP split. Standing still to be leeched, the old way, doesn't work. A big level gap also shrinks the lower player's share: a level 25 hunting with a level 40 gets roughly 30%.

On a new account, trading opens once one character reaches level 15 and a day has passed. Trading rules and the "meso debt" system are in the trading and mesos guide.

Citizenship from level 12

This system is unique to Classic World. At level 12 you can become a citizen of Henesys or Kerning City, and higher ranks make that town's shops, taxis and storage cheaper. When mesos are tight early on, cheaper potions and storage add up, so it pays to get citizenship in the town you hunt near. Ranks and perks are in the citizenship and housing guide.

Other things worth knowing

Right-click a character, or a name in chat, to see their info. On some maps, changing channel puts you back at the map's starting point, which can save a walk back. Many players also find Classic World a little tighter than old Maple: monsters take one more hit and you go through more potions. Expecting that makes the early levels less frustrating.
