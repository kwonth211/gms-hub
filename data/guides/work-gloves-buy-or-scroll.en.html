If a 6 ATT pair of Work Gloves makes you think, “I could make that myself,” add the scroll bill before buying the clean pair. For gloves you need to hunt with, buying finished 6 ATT is the cheaper route. A cheap base item still leaves five upgrade slots to pay for.

We grouped market listings with the same critical rate and weapon defence. Finished 6 ATT started around 4.20m, while a clean pair and five Intermediate scrolls cost at least 8.43m. You spend close to the price of two finished pairs before you know what your one pair will become.

The clean pair was cheap. The scrolls emptied the budget.

The clean item's price is the first tempting number. Once you find a cheap pair, the job feels halfway done: now you only need the scrolls. Buying those one at a time hides how much the five slots cost together.

At the saved median prices, the clean gloves cost about 740k and the five scrolls cost 9.10m. The scrolls consume most of this budget. Finding slightly cheaper clean gloves barely changes the gap to a finished pair.

Open the finished listings first when you are shopping for hunting gear. Then add up the base and scroll prices before spending. Scrolls already in your inventory still have a sale value; owning them does not make them free materials.

The first failure is a stopping point

If you wanted 10 ATT and the first scroll fails, put the next scroll aside. With Intermediate scrolls alone, even passing every remaining slot no longer reaches the target. Trying to recover the first scroll's cost commits more money to the same pair.

Changing the goal to “at least 8 ATT” keeps that spending going. After the first failure, all four remaining scrolls must pass to reach 8. Sell the gloves with four slots remaining and keep the money for your next equipment purchase.

A five-pass result is memorable, but an equipment budget also pays for the failed pairs. Scrolling five pairs all the way at the saved prices costs about 49.20m, and getting no 10 ATT pair is still the more likely outcome. When your purpose is to obtain hunting gear, return to listings with the attack already attached.

After buying 6 ATT, do not rush the next glove upgrade

Once you have 6 ATT, an 8 ATT pair starts looking attractive. Our market groups showed that the same two extra attack points become much more expensive higher up. The jump from 4 to 6 costs far less than the jump from 6 to 8.

Go back to the monster you actually hunt. If new gloves still leave it at two hits, keep money for your weapon or stars. Put the new attack into the Hits-to-Kill Calculator and buy the upgrade that removes an attack first.

Five Lesser scrolls offer a failure-free route to 5 ATT. At the saved prices, though, that route was only about 210k below a finished 6 ATT pair. Save the small difference and buy 6 ATT, without finishing one pair only to start shopping for its replacement.

Leave money for the equipment after these gloves

If 6 ATT is your target, choose from the finished pairs available today. If you enjoy scrolling itself, set aside a separate amount you can lose without delaying your next upgrade. Separating the hunting budget from the scrolling budget makes it easier to stop after a failure.

The prices and arithmetic used here

  • Snapshot: November 28, 2026, 13:59–14:01 KST. Open listings were read within the previous 24 hours; sold figures cover the previous seven days.
  • Gloves: Work Gloves, 6 weapon defence, 2% critical rate. Clean means zero successful upgrades and five slots left; finished means zero slots left.
  • Duplicates: repeated readings with the same seller, channel, room, price and options count once. Listing counts are not stack quantities.
  • Odds: five independent scrolls with a 60% success rate. Exactly k successes: 5Ck × 0.6ᵏ × 0.4⁽⁵⁻ᵏ⁾. At least one 10 ATT pair out of five: 1 − (1 − 0.6⁵)⁵.
  • Rounding: abbreviated prices in the text are rounded. Calculations use the original meso amounts; the sold-price table rounds a half-meso median to the nearest meso.
